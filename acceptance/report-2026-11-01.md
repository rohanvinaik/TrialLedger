## What was selected, and how

Selected 2026-11-01T22:03:35Z (written 2026-11-01T22:05:19Z) by `acceptance/select.py` (SHA-256 `8bb2dda0fdc18c6ac161cbfa36be4de8ca65200309f62f713591f3071d72da5a`), seed 20261101, from the live registry (data 2026-10-30T09:00:04, API 2.0.5; the same before and after the draw: True), before anything was pulled or compared. Criteria, as written before the draw:

> Twenty trials, one per stratum, so that together they span every study type (interventional, observational, expanded access); the phases EARLY_PHASE1, PHASE1, PHASE2, PHASE3, PHASE4 and NA, with two combined-phase records; the overall statuses COMPLETED, TERMINATED, RECRUITING, NOT_YET_RECRUITING, ACTIVE_NOT_RECRUITING, WITHDRAWN, SUSPENDED, UNKNOWN, ENROLLING_BY_INVITATION and the expanded-access statuses; results posted and not; the lead sponsor classes INDUSTRY, OTHER, NIH, FED, NETWORK, OTHER_GOV and INDIV; record size from the smallest of five draws to the largest of five; and at least one record whose identification lists NCT ID aliases. Strata are fixed in this script before any draw; the draws are seeded; nothing about TrialLedger's output informs them.

Each stratum is an Essie expression in `filter.advanced`. A stratum of at most 3000 studies is read whole; a larger one is narrowed to a seeded month of first posting (2000-01 to 2026-09; to its year when the month holds none; up to 12 windows), and one ID is chosen by `random.Random(f'{seed}/{stratum}/choice/{attempt}').choice(sorted IDs)`. Two strata keep the largest or the smallest record (bytes of its JSON) of 5 draws. Every expression, count, window and candidate is in `acceptance/selection.json`.

## What this establishes, and what it cannot

Establishes, for these twenty trials, on the registry's data of 2026-10-30T09:00:04 and TrialLedger at commit `25a90394c5c9213bdf19e35d45e1d82c72f875f2`:

- record.xlsx transcribes the registry's JSON faithfully: every value at the location it names, as the same value of the same kind, and every value of the record exactly once.
- review.xlsx's columns that declare one registry field as their source hold that field's value (or say it is absent where the record holds none); the stated columns that give a row's place hold it; its computed at-risk counts follow their declared rule; its rows stand for the records' elements of each kind once each; and its study, publication and document links are the registry's own forms of them; each with the exceptions named under Differences.
- record.xlsx's Role states nothing the record does not hold: every facet it names is a value of an element enclosing the row's location. The workbooks' About sheets state the registry's API version, data date, the query and the study count as the registry and the selection give them, and account for every requested ID once.
- studies.json holds the records exactly as the registry sends them; studies.csv holds the registry's CSV, with a byte-order mark added.
- The comparisons find each kind of defect planted here (a changed text, number or date, a truncated text, a dropped or duplicated row, a swapped pair, a changed link), so a defect of these kinds in the outputs compared would have been seen.

Cannot establish:

- Anything about clinical meaning: whether a value is clinically right, whether a trial is relevant to a question, or anything the readings of `trialledger resolve` compute (criteria, settings, the profile); they were not compared.
- The registry's own correctness: the reference is the registry's record as the API sent it. Where the registry's CSV departs from its documentation (the Age column), that is recorded, not judged.
- How TrialLedger compares with human transcribers: no person transcribed these trials here; the reader is a program written from the registry's documentation, and its own errors would show as differences or, worse, as agreements it should not have given (the seeded defects bound that risk for the kinds planted, not for every kind).
- Other trials, other data dates or other code: twenty trials across the strata above are a test of the mapping's breadth, not a measured error rate. Columns computed by TrialLedger's rules (other than the at-risk count), record.xlsx's Category, and the fields named under Not comparable were not checked against the registry.
- That the reader chose its pairings independently: which registry field each review.xlsx column holds, the text forms of the c-form columns and the facet names of record.xlsx's Role were transcribed from the workbooks' own About sheets and values. What is independent is resolving each named field in the registry's JSON and comparing; c-declared shows the pairings match the declarations, so a column paired with the wrong field by both would not be seen.
- That a Role names every facet it should: a-role checks that what a Role states holds, not that a row without a facet should have had one.
- Fine order inside the API's CSV columns that join several fields (Age, Study Design, Other IDs, Locations, Study Documents and the outcome columns): (b) checks each documented piece appears somewhere in the cell, so a swap of pieces between two sites or two outcomes in the same cell would not be seen there. The list join ("|") and the Interventions form ("type: name") were read from the CSV itself and are then compared exactly.
- The registry's own page: the comparison is with the API's JSON and CSV, the sources TrialLedger reads, not with the HTML study page.
