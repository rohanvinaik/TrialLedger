# Proposal

## Why

A simulated multistage depression trial exposed lost question-specific scope, unenforceable action prohibitions, disconnected definitions and missing clinical summaries. Retained registry evidence also needs a usable MCP path into source-backed sessions.

## What Changes

- Keep unrelated cohort uncertainty from obscuring warranted entry answers, while retaining global consistency and whole-eligibility checks.
- Add optional declared forbidden-action conditions and bounded concrete case witnesses to finite tables. Coverage remains conditional on the declared variables.
- Add explicit, attributed cross-analysis definition checks: equivalence, implication or disjointness between typed readings bound to actual analysis addresses. Different clinical roles do not imply equality.
- Deliver included table, stage and estimand results in readable Markdown and spreadsheet summaries with exact warrants.
- Expose bounded retained NIH source access and digest-bound source handles through shared CLI/MCP transport, retaining complete hydrated evidence in canonical artifacts.
- Clarify stage/estimand contracts and verify an adversarial, simulated clinical platform against freshly gathered real studies.
- Expose short public typed preparation with inspectable expansion/defaults/origins, eliminating the need for private Python builders in routine design sessions.
- Separate remaining-requirement changes from changed clinical verdicts consistently at option and decision levels.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

None.

### Modified Capabilities

- `symbolic-trial-design`: preserve and connect warranted multistage reasoning through its public clinical workflow.
- `registry-reporting`: expose exact retained source objects without changing the source-faithful report.

## Impact

Scoped entailment and its consumers; finite table checker; session validation/replay/budgets; source-access and reasoning transport; discovery, agent guidance and clinical export. No new dependency or automatic clinical text interpretation.
